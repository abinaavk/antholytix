What Antholytix AI is for

Antholytix AI is a freshness and authenticity check for packaged perishable proteins and dairy — milk, paneer, curd, yoghurt, chicken, meat, fish and similar cold-chain products. Each label carries an anthocyanin pigment strip that shifts colour as the product's surface pH drifts with spoilage, plus a QR code that verifies the label itself is genuine and pulls the product's manufacture and expiry dates.

Where it doesn't apply

The pigment reacts to pH, not to spoilage in general — so it isn't reliable on foods that are already acidic before they spoil (pickles, citrus dishes, fermented foods, vinegar-based preparations) or on dry foods where there isn't enough surface moisture for the pigment to react at all (snacks, grains, dry mixes). It's a freshness and authenticity aid, not a guarantee against every foodborne pathogen — some, like Listeria, can be present without producing the alkaline byproducts the indicator looks for.

Using the scanner

  1. Open the app on your phone (the live camera scanner needs a handheld rear camera, so it's mobile-only by design).
  2. Align the label's pigment square in the first frame — hold steady for a moment while the reading stabilises.
  3. The same pass reads the QR code and looks up the product record.
  4. You get one combined verdict — safe, consume soon, or do not consume — from whichever of the pigment reading or the expiry date is worse.

Sign in to keep a history of what you've scanned — it's tied to your account and only visible to you.

API for manufacturers & retailers

If you're printing Antholytix AI labels for your own products, you register each one against our backend so its QR code resolves to real product data.

Register a label

POST /labels
Authorization: Bearer <your admin token>
Content-Type: application/json

{
  "name": "Fresh Paneer, 200 g",
  "company": "Your Company Pvt. Ltd.",
  "qty": "200 g",
  "mfg": "2026-09-01",
  "exp": "2026-09-09"
}

Returns a generated 16-character code — encode that (or the full JSON) into the label's QR square.

What the scanner calls

POST /scan/analyze
Content-Type: application/json

{ "code": "K7#9-QD4$-N2!B-8XM6", "hue": 342.5, "saturation": 0.61, "value": 0.68 }

Returns the combined pigment + expiry verdict. Full endpoint reference is in the project README.md.

FAQ

Will it work on any food, like lemon rice or leftovers?

No — see What it's for above. It's built for packaged perishable proteins and dairy, not general home cooking.

Why does this need an app instead of just a printed colour key?

The colour difference between "still fine" and "turning" is often subtle enough that a camera reading it numerically is more consistent than eyeballing it under uncertain lighting. The QR side does a different job entirely — proving the label's genuine and checking the date — which a printed legend can't do on its own.

Is my scan history private?

Yes. History and notifications are only visible after you sign in, and the backend scopes them to your account — no one else can see your scans.
